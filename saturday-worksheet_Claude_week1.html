CFB power ratings

Sagarin-style least squares · walk-forward · runs entirely in this tab

Same model as the Python build, ported to JavaScript and checked against it to fifteen decimal places. Nothing leaves your machine and nothing is saved — close the tab and it's gone. Start with the self-test at the bottom; if that passes, the math is right and anything odd afterwards is a data problem.

Column names it understands

Required: home, away, and either (home_score, away_score) or margin. Week is used for recency and walk-forward; if missing everything lands in week 1 and the backtest can't run.

Optional: spread_home (positive when home lays points), close_home, neutral, odds_home, odds_away, date.

Aliases are handled: HOME/AWAY/WK/ASPR/HSPR, home_team/away_team, home_points, spread, closing_spread. Rows with no final score are treated as upcoming games and get predicted rather than trained on.

Pull from CollegeFootballData instead

This calls the API straight from your browser. It may be blocked by CORS depending on how the API is configured that day — if it fails, that's why, and the fix is to pull the data with the Python script or curl and paste it above. Your key is only used for the request and never stored.

5 · Games with no final score

  

  

    Edge is predicted margin minus the spread. Anything under your threshold is not a bet
    — and against a market this sharp, most weeks that's nearly all of them. A row
    marked BET cleared the threshold and had positive EV at the price; it is not a prediction
    that it wins.
  







  
6 · Self-test
no data needed

  

    Builds a fake league with known ratings, a known home field of 2.8 and known noise of 16,
    then checks whether the solver finds them. Also runs the model against a perfectly
    efficient synthetic market, where it should find nothing, and against a market with a
    3-point bias planted in it, where it should find exactly that.
  

  
  






  

    A passing self-test means the estimator is correct. It does not mean the model makes
    money — against real closing lines it will sit at market parity, which is the
    expected result and the reason the bet filter exists.
